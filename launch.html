Step by step

The 6 steps to launch a delivery store.

The same playbook we use on our own brands, like Melt Club in Toronto.

1

Get your accounts approved

Uber Eats and DoorDash ask for your business registration, tax and bank details, ID and food permits. Missing papers are the first reason launches drag on for weeks.

apply through the general form and wait.
we prepare the file with you and use our contacts at both platforms, so you skip the queue.
2

Build a menu made for delivery

A delivery menu is not your counter menu. It's shorter, starts with best sellers and deals, and every item has a reason to add something.

copy the full paper menu, 40+ items, no add-ons.
clear categories, combos, modifiers and upsells that raise the average order.
3

Give every dish the same photo style

Customers decide in seconds. Photos that look like one brand get the tap. Mixed photos look like a store that just opened.

phone photos, different light, some dishes with no photo.
we start from your real dishes and give the whole menu one style. No photographer needed.
4

Price after the commission

The apps take a big cut of each order, often 25 to 30% on a full delivery plan. Prices copied from your counter can leave you working for free.

same prices as in store, margin disappears.
prices set dish by dish so every order still pays you.
5

Set up the store the app rewards

Hours, prep time, cuisine tags and the banner all change how often the app shows you. A slow prep time or the wrong tag hides you from the right customers.

default settings, generic banner.
real prep times, the tags people search, a banner that sells your best dish.
6

Plan your first 14 days

A new store has no ratings yet. The first two weeks decide how the app ranks you. You need the right offer and a small ad budget on the right dishes.

a discount on everything, or nothing at all.
an offer for new customers only and in-app ads, adjusted day by day.
From our own kitchens

Mistakes that kill a new store.

We made most of them ourselves since 2020. You don't have to.

✕Discounts for everyone

Buy-one-get-one on the whole menu brings orders that lose money and customers who never pay full price.

✕A menu that's too long

More choice means slower decisions. Customers leave before they pick.

✕Photos that don't match the food

If what arrives doesn't look like the photo, the bad reviews come fast.

✕A prep time that's too long

The app shows faster kitchens first. A few extra minutes can push you down the list.

✕Rejecting orders at rush

Turn items off before the rush instead. Rejected orders hurt your ranking.

✕Launching and waiting

The first 14 days need someone watching the numbers and fixing what doesn't sell.
